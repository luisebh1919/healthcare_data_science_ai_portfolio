# 30-Day Hospital Readmission Prediction in Adults With Cancer

## Clinical question

Among adult patients with cancer discharged from an inpatient hospitalization, does longitudinal clinical history during the previous 180 days improve prediction of 30-day hospital readmission compared with age and sex alone?

## Project scope

This is a small final project focused on cohort definition, feature construction, and prediction of 30-day hospital readmission. The planned work is intentionally limited to one cohort, six predictors, and three models.

This phase does not include implemented SQL, feature extraction, model training, or results.

## Data

The project will use synthetic patient data generated by Synthea and converted to the OMOP Common Data Model. The synthetic nature of the data must be considered when interpreting any later results.

## Cohort

The cohort will include adults aged 18 years or older with a cancer diagnosis recorded before or on the index date. Patients must have a first eligible inpatient hospitalization, be alive at discharge, have at least 180 days of observable history before discharge, and have at least 30 days of observation after discharge.

The index date is the discharge date of the first eligible inpatient hospitalization. Each patient contributes one row.

## Features

The lookback window is the 180 days before the index date. Planned predictors are:

- age;
- sex;
- number of previous hospitalizations;
- number of distinct diagnoses;
- number of distinct medications;
- number of clinical measurements.

No feature may use information after the index date.

## Models

Three models are planned:

- M0: logistic regression with age and sex;
- M1: logistic regression with all six predictors;
- M2: XGBoost with all six predictors.

## Evaluation

Models will be evaluated using AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, and calibration plots. The main comparison is whether the 180-day clinical history features improve prediction relative to age and sex alone.

## Reproducibility

The project will keep cohort definitions, feature logic, modeling code, and tests in version-controlled files. Data files and local databases are ignored because they are generated or environment-specific.

## Tests

Planned tests will focus on the scientific rules that are easiest to break: one row per patient, correct index date selection, 180-day lookback boundaries, 30-day outcome timing, and prevention of leakage from post-index information.

## Limitations

The data are synthetic. Predictive performance will not imply real clinical utility. There will be no external validation in real patients. The cancer definition will depend on the OMOP concept set used.

## AI use declaration

AI assistants were used to support programming, documentation, and review. Methodological decisions, interpretation, validation, and final responsibility remain with the author.
