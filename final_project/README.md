# 30-Day Hospital Readmission Prediction in Adults With Cancer

## Clinical question

Among adult patients with cancer discharged from an inpatient hospitalization, does longitudinal clinical history during the previous 180 days improve prediction of 30-day hospital readmission beyond prior hospital use?

## Project scope

This is a small final project focused on cohort definition, feature construction, and prediction of 30-day hospital readmission. The planned work is intentionally limited to one cohort, six predictors, and three models.

SQL cohort definitions are implemented in `sql/`, with a final cohort of 824 patients. Feature extraction and model training remain planned.

## Data

The project will use synthetic patient data generated by Synthea and converted to the OMOP Common Data Model. The synthetic nature of the data must be considered when interpreting any later results.

## Cohort

The cohort includes adults aged 18 years or older at index admission with a cancer diagnosis recorded before or on that admission. Patients must have a first eligible inpatient hospitalization, be alive at discharge, have at least 180 days of observable history before admission, and have at least 30 days of observation after discharge.

`index_admission` is the start of the first eligible inpatient hospitalization; `index_discharge` is its discharge. These are distinct temporal anchors. Each patient contributes one row. The readmission outcome is a new inpatient hospitalization beginning after `index_discharge` and within the following 30 days.

## Features

The predictor window is `[index_admission - 180 days, index_admission)`. Planned predictors are:

- age;
- sex;
- number of previous hospitalizations;
- number of distinct diagnoses;
- number of distinct medications;
- number of clinical measurements.

All longitudinal predictors use the 180-day window. Features end strictly before `index_admission`; the index hospitalization contributes no predictor information.

## Models

Three models are planned:

- M0: logistic regression with age, sex, and previous hospitalizations;
- M1: logistic regression with all six predictors;
- M2: XGBoost with all six predictors.

## Evaluation

Models will be evaluated using AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, and calibration plots. The main comparison is whether the 180-day clinical history features improve prediction beyond age, sex, and previous hospitalizations. M2 will be interpreted using global SHAP values, summarized with mean absolute SHAP values and a SHAP summary plot; these explain predictions, not causal effects.

## Reproducibility

The project will keep cohort definitions, feature logic, modeling code, and tests in version-controlled files. Data files and local databases are ignored because they are generated or environment-specific.

## Tests

Planned tests will focus on the scientific rules that are easiest to break: one row per patient, correct index hospitalization selection, 180-day lookback boundaries before admission, 30-day outcome timing after discharge, and prevention of leakage from information recorded at or after index admission.

## Limitations

The data are synthetic. Predictive performance will not imply real clinical utility. There will be no external validation in real patients. The cancer definition will depend on the OMOP concept set used.

AI assistants were used to support programming, documentation, and review. Methodological decisions, interpretation, validation, and final responsibility remain with the author.
